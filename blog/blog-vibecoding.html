Vibecoding: build apps without coding

June 18, 2024 Vibecoding • No-Code • Testing

Vibecoding lets people who have ideas — not necessarily coding skills — launch working applications by describing outcomes. You assemble triggers, integrations, and UI blocks with natural language or a visual canvas while the platform writes the code on your behalf.

Is vibecoding really software?

Yes. The logic still ends up as APIs, workflows, and infrastructure — the difference is that you express it through prompts and configurable building blocks. Tell the system “collect incident details and post them to Slack with a follow-up checklist” and it wires the plumbing without exposing you to YAML or SDKs.

That makes vibecoding ideal for subject-matter experts who know the business flow but not the syntax. Engineers can still review or extend the generated artefacts, yet the first usable version can be delivered in hours instead of sprints.

Why test no-code builds?

Because real users depend on them. I keep a lightweight regression pack that checks validation rules, integration credentials, and fallback paths. Whenever I tweak a prompt, automated smoke tests rerun to ensure the generated logic still behaves as expected.

What you can launch with vibecoding

Recent builds from my lab include:

  • Customer intake portals that push submissions into CRM and open follow-up tasks in Slack.
  • Incident command runbooks where operators pick a scenario and the platform executes the right automation.
  • Metrics dashboards combining API data, AI summaries, and scheduled reports — all delivered without writing code.

No-code doesn’t mean no discipline. Vibecoding keeps the entry barrier low, while DevOps practices keep the results dependable so teams can experiment fast and ship with confidence.
